Distances and times from Bergamo Airport

From Orio al Serio you take the A4 towards Venice: the whole southern shore sits between 75 and 95 km away. For Salò and the Brescia riviera you exit at Brescia and follow the western-shore road; for Limone and the upper lake you continue along the Gardesana Occidentale. Summer weekends add time.

Where we take you on Lake Garda

The lake has two shores — Lombard to the west, Veneto to the east — and one transfer from Bergamo Airport serves both:

  • 🏰 Sirmione — the Rocca Scaligera, a 13th-century moated castle with drawbridge, the Grotte di Catullo (the monumental remains of a 1st-century Roman villa on the tip of the peninsula) and the Aquaria Thermal SPA on the lakefront
  • ⛵ Desenzano del Garda — the lively hub of the lower lake, often called the lake's capital, with the main railway station on the Milan–Venice line
  • 🏛️ Peschiera del Garda — a fortified town on the Mincio whose pentagonal Venetian walls have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2017
  • 🌴 Salò and Gardone Riviera — the elegant western riviera; Gardone is home to the Vittoriale degli Italiani, Gabriele d'Annunzio's hillside estate-museum with the ship Puglia set into the hill
  • 🍷 Bardolino and Lazise — on the Veneto shore: Bardolino is the village behind the DOC wine and its vineyard hills; Lazise is one of the few Garda towns with its medieval walls fully preserved, watched over by the Scaliger castle

Gardaland with kids: door to door, no changes

Gardaland, at Castelnuovo del Garda on the Veneto shore, is Italy's most visited theme park — around 3 million visitors a year. The 2026 season runs from 28 March to 1 November, plus Halloween weekends and Magic Winter dates. From Milan it's about 138–145 km, roughly 1h30 by car (longer on summer weekends); the A4 exit is Peschiera del Garda and the park sits just a few kilometres from the motorway.

By train you reach Peschiera del Garda station, about 2 km from the park, then take Gardaland's free shuttle, every 30 minutes on park opening days. It works — it just isn't door-to-door. With a private transfer the chauffeur collects the whole family at your Milan hotel, or at Malpensa, Linate or Bergamo arrivals, and drops you at the park: child seats on request, pushchairs and bags in the boot.

FAQ

How long does it take from Bergamo Airport to Lake Garda?+
The southern shore is close: Desenzano del Garda ~50 minutes (~75-80 km on the A4), Sirmione ~55 minutes (~85 km), Peschiera and Gardaland about an hour. Allow ~1h for Salò on the Brescia riviera and ~1h35-1h50 for Limone sul Garda along the Gardesana Occidentale. Summer weekends add time.
Will the driver wait if my flight is late?+
Yes. Your flight is tracked in real time: if it is delayed, pickup adjusts at no extra cost. The first hour of waiting from actual landing is included, and your driver meets you at arrivals with a name sign.
Is there a bus from Bergamo Airport to Lake Garda?+
Yes — the Autostradale coach links Orio al Serio to Desenzano in about 120 minutes (€15.50, 12 departures a day); for Sirmione you then need the local LN026 bus (10-12 minutes). There is no direct link to Salò, Gardone Riviera or Limone: changes are required. A private transfer reaches any town on the lake in a single door-to-door ride.
Do you also cover Limone sul Garda and the upper lake?+
Yes: Limone sul Garda (~1h35-1h50), Riva del Garda and every town on both shores. In summer the Gardesana Occidentale gets congested — your driver manages the route and timing while you watch the lake.
How much is a transfer from Bergamo Airport to Lake Garda?+
The price depends on the exact destination, vehicle, time and number of passengers. Message us on WhatsApp with your flight, date and destination: within 2-5 minutes you get a fixed quote agreed in writing that does not change with traffic.
What if I land at Malpensa or Linate?+
We also cover Malpensa and Linate to Lake Garda — from Malpensa the southern shore is about 1h40-2h away. Just mention your arrival airport in the WhatsApp message and you get a quote for your route.
